# THE CJP FILES — Research checkpoint 003
**Reviewed:** 2026-10-19 (India)  
**Scope:** Official orders on Delhi protest-site designation, 2026 CJP custody chronology, preservation of original digital records, and accurate public political affiliations.  
**Editorial status:** Proposed changes, not yet merged; court document reproductions are sourced but not certified by the publisher.

## 1. Primary record: Supreme Court order, 9 October 2026 (S64)

**Citation:** *M A Baby v Union of India*, W.P.(Civil) Diary No. 63560/2026 and W.P.(Cr.) Diary No. 63544/2026, Supreme Court Record of Proceedings dated 9 October. Text mirrored at https://indiankanoon.org/doc/2663624/.

- Paragraphs 3–9 discuss emergency public-transport restrictions affecting metro/rail services and 57 metro stations. The Court described total stoppages as apparently overreaching and directed reassessment in favour of proportional regulation and public advisories.
- **Paragraph 10** proposes that the competent authority consider suitable alternative demonstration venues with basic amenities.
- Paragraph 10 **does not order denotification of Jantar Mantar**. Neither should a recommendation to consider options be treated as a judicial finding that the site was unlawful.
- Paragraph 11 directs reports from Delhi Metro, rail and police-traffic authorities within four days. Final compliance files remain missing.

## 2. Administrative decision chronology: competing legal explanations (S66–S68)

- **20 August:** As reconstructed by Indian Express, MHA forms a committee for assessing protest venue alternatives, with inspections later in August and internal report submitted in September. Original terms, minutes and report have not been obtained.
- **9 October:** Supreme Court hears challenge to severe transport restrictions and suggests consideration of alternate protest sites.
- **11/12 October:** Police standing order/circular, reported as dated 11 October and implemented from 12 October, removes Jantar Mantar from the designated-venue list; names Ramlila Ground, Burari Ground and CBD Ground as alternatives. Retain original document's exact effective date when obtained; contemporaneous reports are not fully consistent.
- **13 October:** Counsel appearing for police tells Delhi High Court the move followed Supreme Court's 9 October order (S66).
- **15 October:** Solicitor General tells Supreme Court that the prior MHA committee recommendation, and not the Court's earlier observations, drove the decision (S67).
- **Finding:** Explanations differ. The conflict is **documented in reporting** but does **not by itself prove malice, procedural invalidity or court contempt**. Read government affidavits and actual notification before assessing administrative legality.

## 3. Primary record: Supreme Court order, 15 October 2026 (S65)

**Citation:** Supreme Court Record of Proceedings dated 15 October 2026 in W.P.(C) 1333/2026 with connected matters, 20-page PDF mirrored at https://www.livelaw.in/pdf_upload/2026/10/16/63560202612275514order15-oct-2026-706811.pdf.

- **W.P.(C) 1357/2026**, the protest-site challenge (item 31), appears in the grouped proceedings for which the Court issued notice.
- For grouped petitions, the Court directs law-enforcement agencies to preserve **CCTV and other digital evidence** collected. Private parties may also file relevant collected material.
- The Union is directed to produce Section 163 BNSS orders applicable to New Delhi, apart from the existing order dated 23 August 2026.
- Nodal counsel are asked to organise cases into categories **on or before 28 October 2026** to facilitate a substantive reply.
- Relevant grouped petitions, including item 31, are listed for **3 November 2026**. Other separately grouped matters have different dates; do not report 3 November as a universal deadline.
- This is **procedural oversight**. It neither rules on the constitutionality of the denotification nor proves alleged protest or police misconduct. Original affidavits, section 163 orders, preserved media and later ROPs are pending.

## 4. Separate custody sequences, not one detention (S69–S73)

- First sequence: Dipke, Das and Ranka were detained on 10 October and released following 11 October SEM proceedings (S72).
- Second sequence: Das and Ranka were re-detained after a 13 October incident and placed in five-day custody by an order dated 14 October, which India Today reproduced in text (S69).
- Reproduced order describes a prior bond, preventive BNSS §§126, 130, 136, 170, police allegations concerning their entry at Jantar Mantar, and the stated absence of an acceptable surety. It is **not a criminal conviction**. The text cannot replace a certified court-file copy or full documentary exhibits.
- The 18 October release hearing reportedly involved competing CJP and police accounts concerning the adequacy and rejection of surety documents. News reports say the SEM withdrew from the hearing after alleging threats (S70/S71).
- Reporter descriptions vary between an **NCR** and **FIR** relating to the alleged messages. No underlying registration, signed recusal order or reliable sender attribution has been secured.
- The status of Das and Ranka after any further proceedings on 19 October was **not confirmed by a signed release or custody order** during this update. Do not invent one.
- NCRB's official BNSS text (S73) provides legal context; the mere invocation of a section does not decide legality of one person's custody.

## 5. Political-affiliation accuracy (S74–S75)

CPI(ML) Liberation's official site lists AISA among its associated organisations, and contemporary Indian Express reporting describes AISA as its student wing, with Neha Bora as national president. The public CJP-AISA 15 October event establishes *event-specific cooperation*. **It does not establish that CPI(ML) owns, bankrolls or issues instructions to CJP.** One new network edge records the actual AISA party affiliation, not an inferred CPI(ML)-CJP relationship.

## 6. Security deployment lead (S76)

Indian Express reports Delhi Police requested extension of 273 of 301 Central Armed Police Forces companies based on anticipated protests. The original police request and actual deployments have not been compared. Treat these as reported requested company numbers, not as evidence of real deployment totals, crowd size, threat actuality or a quantified nationwide operation.

## Assessment

The Court's formal record documents procedural constraints on blanket transport restrictions, a pending venue challenge and directions to safeguard digital evidence. The discrepancies between official explanations require documentary reconciliation; this archive does not prejudge the court's ultimate decision. Documented AISA political affiliations and public CJP cooperation do not substantiate an unlawful covert domestic or foreign command chain.
