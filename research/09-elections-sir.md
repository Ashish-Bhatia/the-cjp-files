# Volume 9. Elections, SIR and the Election Commission
## What the judicial record settles and leaves unresolved

**Research cutoff:** 19 October 2026  
**Editorial status:** First legal-evidence chapter. A nationwide quantitative audit has **not** been completed.

### Central question

Do the courts and public election records support or defeat the disputed claims about Special Intensive Revision (SIR), particularly electoral-roll deletions and the public demands raised by CJP?

The answer has to be broken into **separate proceedings and separate electoral-roll exercises**. One ruling on the validity of a particular exercise cannot settle every future question about its implementation.

## 1. The authoritative 27 May judgment: Bihar, 2025

The [Supreme Court of India's official judgment summary](https://www.sci.gov.in/landmark-judgment-summaries/) identifies:

- **Case:** *Association for Democratic Reforms v. Election Commission of India*
- **Number:** Writ Petition (Civil) 640/2025
- **Citation:** **2026 INSC 564**
- **Date:** **27 May 2026**
- **Bench:** Chief Justice Surya Kant and Justice Joymalya Bagchi.
- **Question:** Legality of the SIR launched in Bihar pursuant to the **24 June 2025** ECI order.

According to the Court's own explanatory summary, the Court **upheld the validity of the Bihar SIR** under Article 324 and section 21(3) of the Representation of the People Act, 1950. The judgment addressed proportionality, voter-roll accuracy, procedural safeguards and the Commission's power to make a **limited inquiry into citizenship for electoral eligibility**. It referred to notice, opportunity to respond, reasoned decisions and appellate routes under the applicable legislation.

The official summary explicitly warns that it **does not form part of the Court's decision or reasoning and cannot be used in legal proceedings**. Therefore, the summary establishes the Court's public account of the ruling, but a fully audited legal chapter must inspect and cite the authentic signed judgment itself with paragraph pinpoints.

The official full-text judgment is linked at:
[Supreme Court judgment PDF, 27 May 2026](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2025/35785/35785_2025_1_1501_71617_Judgement_27-May-2026.pdf). This research edition has **not** obtained or read its full 124-page contents.

### Bihar baseline numbers, with denominators

The Supreme Court's official summary records:

| Measure | Count / date | Meaning and warning |
|---|---|---|
| Bihar pre-SIR roll | 7.89 crore | Registered electors before this particular SIR. |
| Exclusions from initial draft | Nearly 65 lakh, 1 August 2025 | Draft-stage exclusion, **not** automatically the same as permanent disenfranchisement. |
| Bihar final roll | 7.42 crore, 30 September 2025 | Final recorded electors after the exercise. |
| State election | November 2025 | Context for the Bihar-specific lawsuit. |

These are **Bihar 2025** counts, reproduced from the Court's explanatory summary. Subtracting one from another without reconciling additions, corrections, removals and reinstatements is insufficient to prove causes or classify affected voters.

A national 2026 denominator and individual eligibility verification are **not** established by these figures.

## 2. The revised Form 6 dispute is distinct

[The Indian Express reported on 12 October 2026](https://indianexpress.com/article/legal-news/sc-form-6-10918144/) that the Supreme Court clarified a later modified voter-registration **Form 6** had **not been approved or rejected** in the May decision. This narrow clarification matters because the form did not yet exist in its later version when the earlier judgment was delivered.

That is a report of the Court's oral or procedural position, **not** a ruling that the new form is unconstitutional. The original written order, its case number and the precise approved document versions remain pending collection.

## 3. October 2026 judicial proceedings

[Contemporary court reporting](https://indianexpress.com/article/legal-news/supreme-court-seeks-response-of-centre-delhi-police-on-plea-challenging-removal-of-jantar-mantar-as-protest-site-10922726/) describes October court notices to election officials concerning decision-making and records, as well as distinct judicial scrutiny over access to protest sites and reported police actions.

The stage **notice issued / response requested** must not be rewritten as **allegations proved**. A decision to obtain records is not a declaration that the Election Commission acted unlawfully. Conversely, May's ruling on Bihar cannot automatically disprove implementation-level errors in subsequent states.

The signed October orders, parties, consolidated case numbers, affidavits, hearing transcripts and any later rulings are not yet in this register.

## 4. CJP's stated demands and the evidence needed to test them

Reuters' [13 October analysis of protest grievances](https://www.reuters.com/world/india/what-is-fuelling-indias-protests-over-changes-voter-lists-2026-10-13/) and [16 October demonstration reporting](https://www.reuters.com/world/india/security-stepped-up-indias-capital-cockroach-movement-rally-2026-10-16/) place CJP's anti-CEC mobilisation in the context of nationwide electoral-roll controversy.

CJP leaders and allied actors argued that legitimate voters risked exclusion. Election officials and government representatives said the revision was intended to correct duplicate, deceased, shifted or otherwise ineligible entries. Those are competing stated positions, not self-proving factual assessments.

For each state and roll edition, a serious numerical audit requires:

1. Publicly identified state and assembly constituency.
2. Snapshot date and baseline eligible electorate.
3. Draft deletions, additions and pending objections.
4. Reasons for deletion, preserving voter privacy.
5. Appeals, reinstatements and corrected final rolls.
6. Legally established eligibility outcomes, distinguished from administrative status.
7. Source methodology and reasons for inaccessible records.

No nationwide 2026 deletion estimate is presented as independently verified in this edition.

## 5. Provisional conclusions

- **Established from an official Court summary:** On 27 May 2026 the Supreme Court upheld the Bihar 2025 SIR framework within the statutory safeguards described.
- **Not established:** That the Court thereby validated every later nationwide exercise, every deletion or the revised Form 6.
- **Separately disputed:** Whether subsequent ECI procedures and individual exclusions met statutory and constitutional standards.
- **Not yet determined here:** How many eligible citizens were wrongly removed, whether there was intentional partisan discrimination, or whether later remedies were effective.
- **Research task:** Obtain all operative judgments and interim orders, plus ECI state-wise roll datasets before issuing a factual national assessment.
