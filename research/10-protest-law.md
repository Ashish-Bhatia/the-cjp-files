# Volume 10. Law, Protest and Public Order
## Selected October 2026 CJP-related proceedings

**Cutoff:** 19 October 2026. **Status:** Selected reported incidents, with missing case files and interim findings clearly identified. This is **not** a complete national legal ledger or a finding of criminal responsibility.

### Central legal distinction

In Indian protest law, restrictions on time, place and manner of public assemblies require a legal basis and context-specific review. A police allegation, denial of permission, preventive detention and conviction describe different legal events. They cannot be substituted for one another. Equally, reported judicial criticism of a detention does not necessarily establish final civil or criminal liability.

The relevant constitutional starting points are Article 19(1)(a), speech and expression; Article 19(1)(b), peaceful assembly without arms; and Article 19(1)(c), association, subject to permissible restrictions under the Constitution. The outcome of an individual case turns on the applicable statutes, orders, facts, and judicial findings.

## Case LEG-002: The 11 October Panaji detentions

**Place:** Azad Maidan, Panaji, Goa.  
**Issue:** Protest demanding the resignation of Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar.  
**Public reporting date:** 12 October 2026.  
**Reported proceedings:** *Writ Petition (Filing) 2830/2026 and 2831/2026*, High Court of Bombay at Goa. The filing numbers are derived from reporting and await confirmation in the official case-status system.

Seven protesters affiliated with a CJP-supported mobilisation were detained. The [Indian Express' courtroom report](https://indianexpress.com/article/india/goa-high-court-orders-release-of-seven-protesters-cec-gyanesh-kumar-10917166/) says a division bench comprising Justices Amit S. Jamsandekar and Valmiki Menezes directed interim release, observing that detention **may not have been authorised** in the absence of a prohibition order under **section 163 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS)**.

The qualification matters. It was a reported **interim judicial observation and release order**, not a final judgment establishing all claims by the protesters or liability of individual police personnel. [Later reporting](https://www.livelaw.in/high-court/bombay-high-court/after-bombay-high-court-orders-release-of-cjp-members-goa-police-justifies-detention-citing-past-violations-patronising-umar-khalid-554371) says police defended their action, citing prior incidents and statutory preventive powers including BNSS section 172(2). The investigating court's final evaluation and the procedural status on 19 October remain unverified.

**Primary record missing:** Signed interim order, docket, police affidavits, custody registers and subsequent hearing minutes.

## Case LEG-003: Separate FIR reported concerning the Goa event

[The Indian Express reported on 18 October](https://indianexpress.com/article/india/goa-goa-azad-maidan-protest-fir-communal-slogans-cjp-pramod-sawant-10926804/) that Goa Police registered an FIR against **unidentified persons** alleging communal slogans at the 11 October gathering, citing sections **196 and 299 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS)**.

The report describes an accusation and an investigation. It does not establish who raised any specific slogan, whether the entire scene is authentically represented in circulating videos, or whether the statutory elements of any offence were proved. The separate legality issue concerning detention does not determine the truth of the later FIR. The FIR itself, complainant statement, provenance of footage and progress of any chargesheet have not been obtained. Ordinary attendees must not be individually linked to the allegation without direct evidence.

**Primary record missing:** FIR number, registration time, authenticated source footage, investigation status and any signed subsequent judicial orders.

## Case LEG-001: The 13 October Delhi protest detentions

On 13 October, CJP public officers **Saurav Das** and **Ashutosh Ranka** reportedly entered the Jantar Mantar area for a protest concerning the Chief Election Commissioner. [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/india/indian-police-detain-cockroach-movement-leaders-demanding-election-chiefs-2026-10-13/) and [The Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/cities/delhi/cjp-ashutosh-ranka-saurav-das-and-ajinkya-shinde-preventive-arrest-jantar-mantar-protest-10920696/) reported police intervention and five-day custody.

Police and protest organisers gave different accounts of the justification for the measure. References to denotification of the area, prior undertakings, preventive powers and judicial custody require separate inspection of the operative administrative order, police grounds and Special Executive Magistrate proceedings. The existence of a detention does not prove the underlying alleged prohibited conduct.

**Primary records missing:** Custody and arrest documentation, Special Executive Magistrate court case number, statutory charge or bond provision, Jantar Mantar denotification notification, signed custody order and later outcome.

## Delhi 16 October: Connaught Place demonstration

[Reuters' 16 October report](https://www.reuters.com/world/india/security-stepped-up-indias-capital-cockroach-movement-rally-2026-10-16/) documents police restrictions and detentions around a tricolour protest. Public demonstrations by CJP, political parties, students and regional organisations over the same electoral issue must be catalogued as separate events unless an organiser's joint authority is supported by direct sources.

[Reuters subsequently reported Dipke's release](https://www.reuters.com/world/india/indias-cockroach-movement-founder-arrested-media-reports-say-2026-10-17/). Release conditions, formal court jurisdiction, alleged violations and the latest legal status require the actual orders.
