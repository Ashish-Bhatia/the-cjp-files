## A. Direct legal record: 1 September 2026

**S54** is a text mirror of the Supreme Court's Record of Proceedings, W.P.(Civil) No. 917/2026, listed with W.P.(Criminal) No. 280/2026. Relevant passages: paras 2, 7, 10–15 of the order.

- The Delhi Police, Bihar, West Bengal, Assam, and Maharashtra applied for Article 142 relief regarding FIRs arising from July student protests. The order explicitly lists 13 Delhi FIRs and records several State FIR lists.
- Paragraph 13(i) quashes the listed FIRs. Paragraphs 13(ii)–(iii) close/block FIRs for the **same incidents** between 20 and 25 July 2026, subject to an exception, so this is not a lifetime immunity for participants.
- Paragraph 13(iv) permits a **fresh FIR concerning the 2,873 persons described in the police application**, preserving affected persons' remedies. It is **not a finding that 2,873 identified faces were correctly matched, that each person attended the protest, or that anyone was convicted**.
- Paragraph 13(v) directs the Centre to formulate a compensation-policy/arrangement within 90 days and make payment thereafter. **The order alone does not prove policy implementation or payments**.
- Paragraph 11 records CJP co-convenor Saurav Das stating that the announced 5 September march would be withdrawn in light of the commitments.
- The text notes the court's Article 142 use is conditioned on compliance with the settlement and is not to be used as binding precedent.
- **Scope nuance**: Listed FIRs include dates outside the expressed 20–25 July window. Do not silently assume that the general incident window overrides the separately enumerated FIR relief. Obtain court-certified copy and counsel review for a comprehensive interpretation.

## B. Facial-recognition challenge

**S55** reports the 17 August Delhi Police affidavit: 2,873 claimed matches, 2,402 attributed to the police Crime Kundli database and 471 to other criminal records according to contemporaneous reporting. Police argued automated matches require field verification.

**S56** reports an independent check of 205 serious-charge entries and at least 25 alleged protest-site identifications associated with individuals already in jail. It cites court, prison, and police records and describes the dates of claimed matches as 24–26 July. Delhi Police told the newspaper further verification was pending.

**Strict interpretation:**
- `at least 25` is an **independently published journalistic cross-check**, not a separate reproduction by this research team.
- `25 of 205 checked` is **not** the false-match rate among all 2,873 or the accuracy rate of the biometric model, since the 205 were not established as a random representative sample.
- A police database match does not establish physical presence; a prior criminal record or accusation does not prove new criminal conduct; permission to investigate does not validate each match.
- Request the actual police affidavit, FRS model/version, threshold, image metadata, processing/export logs, human verification records, custody certificates, and procedural basis for collection, retention and onward sharing. Protect unnecessary personal identifiers in public releases.
