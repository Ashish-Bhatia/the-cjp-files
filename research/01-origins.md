# Volume 1. The Origin Story
## A foundational investigation of the Cockroach Janta Party

**Research cutoff:** 19 October 2026  
**Publication status:** Foundational draft; not exhaustive  
**Evidence rule:** Contemporary reporting establishes publication, attributed statements and observed public acts. It does not automatically verify speakers' allegations.

### Central question

When did the Cockroach Janta Party (CJP) first appear, who publicly initiated it, and what caused a satirical online publication to become an organised protest movement?

### Finding 1: The dispute preceded the movement

On **15 May 2026**, oral observations attributed to Chief Justice Surya Kant during a Supreme Court proceeding became the subject of a public controversy. In subsequent reporting, the oral remarks appeared to characterise some unemployed young people or online activists using the image of cockroaches. On **16 May**, the Chief Justice said that this broad interpretation misrepresented what he intended: his criticism, he said, had been aimed at people using false qualifications to enter law and other professions, not at Indian youth as a whole.

This origin story requires two records kept apart: (1) what contemporary journalists reported the court heard, and (2) the Chief Justice's subsequent explanation. Neither the full authenticated court recording nor a transcript of the oral exchange was obtained in this research session. It would be unsound to represent one contested account of the speaker's intent as a final judicial finding.

**Sources:** [The Indian Express, clarification report, 16 May](https://indianexpress.com/article/legal-news/cji-surya-kant-clarifies-cockroaches-remark-fake-degrees-row-10692979/); [The Indian Express, historical explainer, first published 24 May](https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/cockroach-janta-party-founder-motive-bjp-congress-details-10705858/).

### Finding 2: Dipke is the earliest independently reported initiator

Multiple reports identify **Abhijeet Dipke**, a communications professional then studying in the United States, as the person who launched the satirical **Cockroach Janta Party** online on **16 May 2026**. This is the earliest *independently reported CJP launch date* established in this review, not yet the earliest directly authenticated digital artifact. Retrospective Times of India reporting attributed a short founding post to Dipke, but the first-party May 16 permalink and an archive with verifiable publication metadata remain outstanding.

The spelling **Cockroach Janta Party** is used here as the main label. **Cockroach Janata Party** appears in reporting and is retained as a search alias, without presuming a separate entity.

**Sources:** [NDTV, 22 May](https://www.ndtv.com/india-news/cockroach-janta-party-who-is-abhijeet-dipke-founder-of-online-sensation-cockroach-janta-party-11531319/amp/1); [Times of India, 26 July retrospective](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/delhi/one-post-6-words-37-days-one-resignation-how-cockroaches-crawled-into-corridors-of-power/amp_articleshow/132626123.cms); [The Indian Express, May chronology](https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/cockroach-janta-party-founder-motive-bjp-congress-details-10705858/).

### Finding 3: Satire coexisted with concrete political demands

The early website, as reproduced or described by The Indian Express before its reported restriction, called itself a platform for disaffected young people. Its satirical presentation was explicit. It also listed substantive demands relating to judicial appointments after retirement, protection of eligible votes, reservation for women, independent media and restrictions on political defection. These are **reported first-party claims reproduced in contemporary journalism**. A full archived original website and evidence establishing that its content was stable over time have not yet been authenticated.

In an attributed interview, Dipke said his intention was to change political discourse and encourage accountability to young people. That attributed objective has stronger evidentiary standing than an outsider's reconstruction of a concealed motive.

**Source:** [The Indian Express, May explainer and interview references](https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/cockroach-janta-party-founder-motive-bjp-congress-details-10705858/).

### Finding 4: The first organisational transition was public

The movement's first reported press conference took place at the **Constitution Club of India, New Delhi, on 3 June 2026**. It publicly identified **Saurav Das** as chief spokesperson, **Vijeta Dahiya** as spokesperson and **Ashutosh Ranka** as spokesperson. The group announced a forthcoming student protest on 6 June, called for accountability for examination irregularities and invited individuals across political affiliations to participate without party banners.

This is a documented public organisational act. It does not establish the rules governing internal membership or demonstrate that each invited party joined.

**Source:** [The Indian Express, report updated 4 June; photographs and first-party post dated 3 June](https://indianexpress.com/article/india/cockroach-cjp-spokespersons-saurav-das-abhijeet-dipke-jantar-mantar-protest-10722635/).

### Finding 5: Reported protests show a transition from media to mobilisation

On **6 June 2026**, Reuters reported a public demonstration in New Delhi led by Dipke after his return from the United States. The protest demanded Union Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan's resignation amid examination irregularities. Reuters reported Pradhan's resignation on **22 July**, but a [25 July presidential communiqué](https://www.presidentofindia.gov.in/press_releases/press-communique-35) formally accepted it with immediate effect under Article 75(2). The publication date of a news report must therefore be separated from the dated constitutional act. The available record supports chronological association and CJP's publicly observed mobilisation. It does **not** by itself prove that CJP alone caused a ministerial resignation. Independent student organisers and the government's own decision-making chronology must be reconstructed before assigning relative causal weight.

**Sources:** [Reuters, 6 June](https://www.reuters.com/world/india/indias-cockroach-youth-movement-founder-arrives-new-delhi-protest-modi-2026-06-06/); [Reuters, 22 July](https://www.reuters.com/world/india/whats-behind-indias-cockroach-youth-protests-2026-07-22/); [President of India, 25 July primary communiqué](https://www.presidentofindia.gov.in/press_releases/press-communique-35).

### Finding 6: National roles were formally announced in August

After a reported two-day meeting in Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar, CJP announced its **National Working Committee on 6 August 2026**. Contemporary coverage reproduced its first leadership list. Positions were *publicly announced*; this investigation has not yet checked internal bylaws, signed appointment documents, decision-making records or any legal incorporation.

Some reports have inconsistent spellings of names; the original organisational statement is the required authority for correction. An appointment to a role called *finance lead* is not evidence of disclosed donations or an operative funding channel.

At this meeting CJP explicitly described itself as a **public pressure group rather than an electoral political party**, and Das said the organisation was **not collecting funds** at that time. This is a dated representation, not a perpetual guarantee or an audited finding. The Election Commission's current registration records and public financing disclosures require independent inspection.

### Finding 7: Public political contact is not proof of party control

In early interviews, Dipke acknowledged past Aam Aadmi Party (AAP) involvement from **2020 to 2022**. The Indian Express separately reported his earlier political communications work. The relevant question is whether this historic association caused present organisational direction or resource support. No funding transfer, present command agreement or verified appointment from AAP to CJP has been obtained here.

In October, Congress contacts were reported. On **15 October**, The Indian Express reported an effort by Congress figures to invite Dipke to a gathering hosted by Rahul Gandhi. The same report says CJP did **not** participate in the INDIA bloc's separate 12 October Supreme Court march. These facts should be represented as **reported attempted contact** and **separate mobilisation**, not as a joint command chain.

**Sources:** [The Indian Express, early May interview](https://indianexpress.com/article/political-pulse/cockroach-janta-party-threats-family-hounded-opinion-10703302/); [The Indian Express, Congress approach, 15 October](https://indianexpress.com/article/political-pulse/cockroach-janta-party-cjp-congress-abhijeet-dipke-rahul-gandhi-high-tea-10922245/).

### Finding 8: October creates a distinct electoral and legal phase

By October, reported CJP protests increasingly concerned **Special Intensive Revision (SIR)** of electoral rolls and demands directed at Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar. This is a change in the movement's publicly emphasised issue focus. The truth of the underlying electoral-roll allegations must be tested separately against ECI records, denominators and court decisions.

Contemporary reporting documents several distinct incidents: an attempted October 10 protest and detentions in Delhi; an October 11 event by a CJP affiliate in Panaji, Goa; an October 13 Jantar Mantar demonstration followed by reported custody of Saurav Das and Ashutosh Ranka; and the October 16 Connaught Place mobilisation. The Indian Express reported a police FIR against *unidentified* persons over allegedly communal slogans at the October 11 Goa protest, and also reported a High Court direction to release seven detainees. Neither the allegation in the FIR nor an initial police detention establishes criminal responsibility.

This phase cannot be fully assessed until the **actual FIRs, preventive custody orders, magistrate orders and signed High Court records** are obtained.

**Sources:** [Reuters, 13 October](https://www.reuters.com/world/india/indian-police-detain-cockroach-movement-leaders-demanding-election-chiefs-2026-10-13/); [Reuters, 16 October](https://www.reuters.com/world/india/security-stepped-up-indias-capital-cockroach-movement-rally-2026-10-16/); [The Indian Express, Goa FIR, 18 October](https://indianexpress.com/article/india/goa-goa-azad-maidan-protest-fir-communal-slogans-cjp-pramod-sawant-10926804/); [The Indian Express, reported Delhi custody](https://indianexpress.com/article/cities/delhi/cjp-ashutosh-ranka-saurav-das-and-ajinkya-shinde-preventive-arrest-jantar-mantar-protest-10920696/).

## Initial conclusions

1. **Founded as satire:** The reported launch and the earliest expressed mission support a satirical origin. Authentication of the original post remains open.
2. **Developed public organisation:** The June press event, street protest and August national appointments provide documented evidence of institutional development.
3. **Political relationships require differentiation:** Dipke's former AAP association, later Congress outreach and student-group participation are **different types of evidence**. None alone proves party control.
4. **Funding is not established:** August statements say fundraising was not taking place; an audited historical funding picture remains unavailable.
5. **Legal status requires records:** Reported detentions, alleged offences and court intervention are not a substitute for signed primary orders.
6. **International comparisons are not causal evidence:** Published Gene Sharp methods describe widely used protest practices; similarity does not prove a CJP relationship with the Albert Einstein Institution, Otpor or CANVAS.

## Record integrity and limitations

The accompanying [machine-readable research register](data.json) contains source IDs, dated public-role records, a selective event ledger, relationship classes, claims and backlog items. It is *not* a nationwide complete census. The research examined selected English-language and Hindi-language sources and concentrated on nationally reported events plus Delhi, Maharashtra and Goa. A primary presidential communiqué is collected. No original court order, primary financial ledger or complete all-state enumeration has been collected.

Corrections should identify the precise claim, replacement evidence, original publication date and changed assessment. Do not add private addresses, unpublished personal records or unsupported personal allegations.
