The people behind the headlines

The individuals most clearly visible in the evidence, and what their public participation does and does not establish.

Abhijeet Dipke

Dipke is repeatedly identified as founder and principal public voice of CJP. Reuters profiled his emergence from online political commentary to protest leadership, while multiple independent reports place him at rallies or note his detention. He is a material public actor because of direct organising and public statements, not because of personal background. S35↗ S02↗ S12↗ S18↗

In Jaipur on 15 June, Dipke was reported assaulted as he entered the gathering. Police reportedly arrested five suspects. That is a documented allegation of an attack on an organiser, not evidence of its motive or its ultimate court outcome. S04↗

Biographical reporting also identifies Dipke's earlier work or volunteering with the Aam Aadmi Party's digital communications and election campaigns, reportedly from 2020 to 2023. That is a material reported historical political affiliation. It does not establish present AAP command of CJP, and payroll or personnel records have not been obtained. S42↗ S43↗

Saurav Das and Ashutosh Ranka

Das publicly spoke about preparations for the 10 October campaign and the risk of arrests; Ranka was a named spokesperson during the July negotiations. Both were reported detained after entering the Jantar Mantar area on 13 October and later remanded. The known procedural status is not a conviction, and the complete custody paperwork remains outside this archive. S30↗ S07↗ S15↗ S16↗

Student and other public actors

Neha Bora of AISA and Aishe Ghosh of SFI were named or pictured in publicly reported demonstrations and the 15 October press conference. AAP leaders including Atishi, along with other opposition and civil society figures, participated in protests against the Election Commission. Their attendance or public endorsement is evidence of visible convergence, not proof that their organisations founded CJP or financed every activity. S11↗ S17↗

Public figures associated with earlier movements, including JNU activists and defendants in historical cases, do not acquire a connection to CJP by reputation alone. No contemporary dossier is published for a historical figure without direct event-specific evidence. This rule also applies to officials accused by movement speakers.

Fairness test

For every individual, the relevant questions are observable decisions, announced office, signed documents, public meetings and verifiable acts. The investigation excludes addresses, family details and speculative profiles of ordinary marchers. Serious allegations require their answer and the underlying records before stronger conclusions are drawn.

Source record & limitations

Source labels open the underlying publisher's document. Published reports and attributed statements are distinguished from original official records. No source is described as independently authenticated when it has not been obtained.
