Historical comparisons

What parallels explain.

The comparison begins with observable public methods, then separates differing institutions, histories and outcomes.

2011 · India

India Against Corruption

Anti-corruption demands, large public demonstrations, political pluralism and the later emergence of the Aam Aadmi Party are a useful domestic baseline.

1974–75 · India

Jayaprakash Narayan movement

The JP movement shows the historical role of students, anti-establishment mobilisation and coalition politics in a very different constitutional context.

2020–21 · India

Farmers' protests

Extended demonstrations, visible organisational leadership and negotiations with government. Shared civil methods do not identify common sponsors.

Aragalaya

Important distinction

Method ≠ instruction

People can independently arrive at flags, satire, slogans or rallies. A direct organisational link demands documents showing actual contact, training, finance or instructions.

Where CJP fits and where it does not

CJP's May satirical posts, June press activity and street demonstrations, and October flag displays resemble long-established methods of public persuasion and assembly. Comparisons with Otpor!, the 2011 Indian anti-corruption movement or Aragalaya are therefore legitimate at the level of observed method.

A more demanding claim is that CJP's organisers were directly trained or directed by an international institution. Shared vocabulary and matching tactics do not establish that claim. Published sources reviewed for this comparison do not establish a documented transfer of operational instructions or finance from the Albert Einstein Institution or CANVAS to CJP.

Comparisons illustrate possibilities and limitations. No foreign organisation is assigned a clandestine role on the basis of resemblance or contact with an unrelated third party.
