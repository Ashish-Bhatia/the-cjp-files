Field reporting / August–September 2026

Who may inspect
a public school?

CJP's School Thik Karo campaign brought classroom facilities, sanitation, school access and citizen oversight into a public dispute. The available record proves some visits and disagreements. It does not verify every audit claim or prove repairs followed from CJP's intervention.

The campaign and its original demand

CJP launched School Thik Karo on 15 August 2026, starting with a village-school inspection in Santuk Pimpri, Hingoli district, Maharashtra. The public-facing checklist asks volunteers to document water, toilets, electricity, classroom safety, boundary walls, midday meals and teacher attendance, then communicate findings to authorities. Reporting described broken windows, sanitation concerns and insufficient basic facilities at the initial school. Those are recorded inspection allegations, not a certified state-wide survey. The Indian Express, 16 August · Campaign's own instructions

A report on 17 August quoted co-convenor Ashutosh Ranka estimating 1,200–1,500 volunteers. That estimate is attributed to the movement and has not been reconciled against a public register of completed, validated inspections. Similarly, the campaign website's school map and national counts should not be treated as proof that every listed school was visited. Careers360, 17 August

The central dispute: transparency versus classroom protection

CJP's stated position is that visiting public schools and recording deficiencies helps communities hold authorities accountable. Officials in Mumbai and Gautam Buddh Nagar cited permission requirements, safety and educational disruption. Neither side's institutional interest settles the precise legal right to enter a campus with children present. The applicable school-specific order, visitor policy and consent requirements matter. An inspection team is not a statutory school inspector merely because it calls its exercise an audit.

Any reliable inspection should record the school identifier, date, checklist version, precise observation, school administration's response, available administrative data, and follow-up after a reasonable correction period. Child identity and private information should be protected. A government's capital-spending announcement made near a visit does not prove the movement caused it; an independent programme and its prior approval may explain the timing.

Further documentary checks

Campus access permissions and refusals

Compare dated municipal or district instructions, the visiting team's written requests, principals' notes and any visitor logs. Consider child safety and disruption alongside civic scrutiny.

Actual infrastructure conditions and outcomes

Check dated before-and-after photographs without identifying children, UDISE+ school records, inspection reports, work orders, completion certificates and publicly available payment records. Treat each facility separately.

Scale and national claims

Request de-duplicated school identifiers and dates. Distinguish platform records, volunteer sign-ups, submitted checklists, site visits and independently verified outcomes. No inference from one category to another.

The public evidence supports a citizen-audit campaign and specific disputes over access. The scale of completed inspections, the completeness of reported deficiencies and causal claims about repairs require school-level corroboration.
