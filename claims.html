The test

What stands up?

Each assessment links the original sources. The finding describes the evidence, not the political or moral standing of its speaker.

A statement is sometimes verifiable only as an assertion made by an identifiable person. That is different from verifying its contents. An absence of proof is not necessarily proof of absence.

Why disputed figures need denominators

Electoral-roll totals are regularly described as deletions, exclusions or disenfranchisement. Those words are not interchangeable. A sound comparison distinguishes the old roll from a new draft, the draft from the final roll, and both from individual determinations of voter eligibility. For the detailed legal context, read the SIR and ECI report.

What the court record changes

The 1 September Supreme Court order is unusually precise: it quashed enumerated July protest FIRs, with a carve-out for a possible new investigation and conditions tied to commitments made before the court. Both “all charges cleared” and “nothing changed” would misstate that document.
