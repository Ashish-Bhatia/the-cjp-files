Is regime change the endgame?

A serious question requires a strict distinction between election politics, constitutional resignation demands and unlawful coercion.

What demonstrators publicly demanded

The record also shows that CJP issued a 48-hour ultimatum on 24 September demanding the Chief Election Commissioner's resignation, threatening further nationwide protest. The demand merits analysis for its institutional implications, but a political ultimatum and even a proposal to suspend scheduled elections are not themselves evidence of a plan to overthrow government by force. The relevant test remains the precise demand, lawful procedure and any verified coercive acts. Reuters also reported the Election Commission's denial of partisan manipulation. S39↗

The earliest large CJP gatherings centred on examinations, recruitment and the resignation of Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan. He resigned in July and the movement suspended its agitation after negotiations. In October, its stated focus shifted to electoral rolls and the resignation of Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar. Those changes in demands are independently documented. S03↗ S07↗ S10↗

Seeking a minister's or election commissioner's resignation is a political demand. Organising rallies, joining an opposition march or trying to win elections is categorically different from using unlawful force to remove a government. The review therefore separates changes in policy, constitutional personnel changes, electoral competition and extra-constitutional coercion.

The strongest primary record of direct interaction with institutions is the Supreme Court's 1 September order. It documents CJP co-convenor Saurav Das publicly withdrawing the September 5 march after an Article 142 settlement with government participants concerning July protest FIRs and compensation. Documented negotiation and concessions support the conclusion that the movement had recognised representatives capable of altering a planned mobilisation. They do not establish a covert sponsor, operational direction by opposition parties, or an unlawful objective. S54↗

The strongest case for strategic political coordination

CJP demonstrably chose national protest dates, attracted student participation and appeared alongside opposition actors. It promoted a joint press conference with AISA on 15 October. These observations warrant scrutiny of campaign planning and organisational relationships. They also have ordinary explanations: aligned political objectives, open coalition work and an attempt to widen a mass protest. S30↗ S17↗

A claimed foreign-donor operation and a claimed state-financed operation are also on record. Neither has yielded a verifiable transaction in the sources reviewed. They cannot presently carry a conclusion of covert destabilisation. S22↗ S38↗

The strongest contrary evidence

The movement's first documented grievances were concrete examination disputes. Its July negotiations ended in withdrawal rather than an announced plan to seize institutions. Its leaders' stated calls for peaceful protest coexist with documented disorder, which needs act-by-act attribution instead of collective criminalisation. S02↗ S07↗ S34↗

Public cooperation with student federations and politicians is not evidence of a secret command channel. Gene Sharp and CANVAS are relevant methodological comparisons, but publicly available tactics do not establish operational instruction. S17↗ S23↗ S25↗

What would change the finding

A sound finding of an unlawful destabilisation operation would require verifiable planning and intent, a specific unlawful objective, and operational steps attributable to identified actors: dated orders, authentic communications, corroborated payments tied to illegal acts, or reliable testimony reinforced by documents. Proven violent offences would need to be evaluated for their scope and connection to any overarching plan.

The current finding is narrower: the CJP-led campaign became politically consequential and visibly cooperative with other groups. Within the public records reviewed for this edition, a concealed foreign-directed or extra-constitutional regime-change operation has not been demonstrated. This is not a declaration that every allegation is disproved.
