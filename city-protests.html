October 16–17 / A four-city comparison

One protest call.
Different outcomes.

Delhi, Mumbai and Pune reported CJP-linked flag demonstrations. A contemporaneous Bengaluru anti-SIR rally involved other student organisers. Location, participation, authorisation and policing differed; none should be inferred from a single national crowd claim.

FIELD REPORTING / ORGANISER ATTRIBUTION / CROWD ESTIMATES
A planned rally is not proof of a completed gathering. “Attended,” “detained” and “arrested” are different measures. Newsroom crowd estimates depend on observation time and geographic boundaries. A protest on the same issue does not establish that CJP directed it.

Why venue permissions change the outcome

In Pune, CJP moved to the Collectorate area after an unsuccessful request for space at Savitribai Phule Pune University. Its local representatives reported receiving permission for the revised venue. The subsequent rally concluded without detentions. Venue reporting, 16 October ↗ · Organisers' permission claim ↗

Mumbai police directed participants to Azad Maidan rather than the Gateway of India, according to the CJP representative interviewed by The Times of India. Delhi Police had separately removed Jantar Mantar from the list of official protest locations, while central Delhi gatherings faced new police restrictions. Bengaluru police cited a Karnataka High Court-linked restriction concerning protest locations and refused permission for Town Hall. These examples show different official justifications, not a controlled experiment proving one specific institution caused all variation.

The analytical boundaries

Size: A crowd count for an hour, a police detention total and attendance across an entire day are not directly comparable. Reuters' Delhi account, the Mumbai reporter's fluctuating attendance and the Pune estimate should each retain their original date, site and methodology.

Organisation: CJP, AISA, Jawab Do and visiting political representatives are distinct actors. Shared demands against SIR do not, without direct evidence, demonstrate a common command chain, financial relationship or formal political coalition.

Claims: Protesters alleged that roll deletions threaten voting rights; the Election Commission rejected accusations of bias. Neither political position is independently resolved by an event's turnout. Examine the electoral-roll evidence separately.

Open questions and records

Authorisations and venue restrictions

Obtain each city's dated permission request, written grant or rejection, grounds of refusal and applicable public-order instrument. Reconcile planned and actual routes rather than presuming the same conditions everywhere.

More reliable crowd estimates

Find time-stamped independent images and video with defined site boundaries, keeping participants' private identities protected. Do not infer national membership or popular support from one site count.

Detentions versus arrests

For each reported figure, request a police station-level tally with legal authority, detention period, release status and any pending case. Preserve the distinction between short preventive detention and custody following a court order.

Evidence assessment: The 16–17 October record proves multiple city-specific demonstrations and different policing outcomes. It does not independently verify a single nationwide attendance figure, common legal status for all participants or unified CJP command of separate student groups.
